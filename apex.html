    The whole strategy, start to finish

    1. Before the bellDana loads the price history. Rex counts overnight headlines and earnings reports. Eve reads yields, the dollar, gold and oil, and checks for a central-bank decision. Ola reads the fear gauges.
    2. 09:30 to 09:35: waitNobody trades in the first five minutes. Those minutes are only measured: how much each stock trades and how far it moves.
    3. 09:35: find the busy stocksSky ranks all halal stocks by opening volume against their 14-day normal. Only the top 5 that trade at least 3 times normal are "in play". On most days that is zero to three stocks.
    4. 09:35 onward: wait for the breakoutSami watches the in-play stocks whose first five minutes went up. When a 1-minute candle closes above the high of those five minutes, he proposes to buy.
    5. The Judge's gatesRefused if it is after 15:30, if the account already holds the stock, or if Wes says the market is BEARISH or HIGH VOL.
    6. The Guardian's size and stopStop one tenth of the stock's normal daily range below the buying price. Position at most 30% of the account. At most 3 positions, 6 new trades a day, and no new trades after losing 1% in a day.
    7. The orderTess buys at the next minute's opening price. If the stop is touched she sells there. There is no profit target: winners are held.
    8. 15:55: everything is soldNothing is ever held overnight. Sara records the day.

    Are we really using all the data?

    No. All of it is collected and shown, but only two things decide a trade. Everything else was tested as a deciding rule and did not help consistently, so it reports and does not vote. Letting unproven inputs vote is how a formula ends up fitted to luck.

    Words used on this page

    Candle: the price movement during one minute: where it started, its highest and lowest point, where it ended, and how many shares traded. In play: a stock trading far more than usual in the first 5 minutes. Opening range: the high and low of the first 5 minutes. VWAP: the average price paid today, weighted by how much traded at each price. Stop: the price where a losing trade is closed automatically. R: one R is the amount risked on a trade; +2R means it made twice what it risked. Profit factor: money won divided by money lost; above 1 is profitable. Slippage: the gap between the price you wanted and the price you got. Fills are simulated at the next minute's opening price, a little worse than the market, with US selling fees. No real money and no broker orders are involved.
