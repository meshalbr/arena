The Night Trade

Buy five stocks just before the US market closes, sell them the next morning at the open, repeat every trading day. It is the only daily model in this project that passed its blind tests. Below: the rule, the honest results month by month, the risks, and the live record.

The rule in plain words

  1. The list: the 100 biggest, most traded halal stocks.
  2. Every trading day, before 15:50 New York time: for each stock, look at what it earned overnight (yesterday's close to today's open) on each of the last 120 days.
  3. Pick ten: the 5 stocks with the best average overnight gain, and the 5 with the steadiest overnight gain. 10 % of the money in each.
  4. Buy them in the closing auction. Sell them in the next opening auction. Auction orders pay no gap between buy and sell prices.
  5. Market filter: if the whole market (SPY) is under its 200-day average, stay in cash that night.
  6. Brake: if the last 20 nights were rougher than usual, invest a smaller share.

Why it works, as far as research can tell: big stocks earn almost all of their long-run gain overnight and almost nothing between the open and the close, and each stock keeps its overnight habit for months (Lou, Polk, Skouras 2019).

The analysis desk: 18 methods, each made to prove itself

Every night the 15 best candidates were examined with the usual tools of chart, volume and market analysis. For each tool the table shows the candidates' result the next morning when the condition was true and when it was false, over 30,465 candidate-nights. A tool is kept only if it points the same way in four separate parts of the data and the gap is too big to be luck.

Lesson kept for the notes: the first run of the learners looked far better (+2.8 % a month) because three measurements quietly used the day's closing price, which is not known when the order is sent. With that removed the advantage was gone.

The aggressive versions

Asked for on 14 November 2026: go big when the analysis is strong. Five versions were written down first and tested in the four separate parts of the data. Three passed (more profit than the blend in all four parts, and at least as much profit per unit of pain in three).

  • Aggressive, no check: each night the 3 best overnight gainers among the 50 most traded stocks, a third of the money in each.
  • Aggressive, trend-checked: the same three, but a stock under its own 200-day average is skipped and its third stays in cash.

They earn about a point a month more than the blend and swing about twice as hard: single nights of −10 % happened. The brake did not help them. Press the buttons at the top to see their months.

On a real small account: whole shares only

Auction orders cannot buy part of a share, and with ten slots a $5,000 account has only about $400 to $500 per slot. Six picks in ten cost more than $200 a share. How it was tested

  • Fixed first, tested after. The rule was written down on 2021–2026 data, then run once on data it had never seen.
  • Blind test 1: the same 100 stocks on 2016–2020. Passed.
  • Blind test 2: 30 stocks chosen back in 2016, prices from a different source, 2017–2026. Passed, 10 of 10 years up.
  • Blind test 3, the honest list used on this page: the companies that were in the S&P 500 on each 1 January, as they were then, including those that later fell or were bought. The 100 most traded each year. Passed.
  • Upgrades (blend, brake) were accepted only if they won in four separate pieces of that list: two halves of the stocks that share no company, each in 2017–2021 and 2022–2026.
  • 48 different settings (list size, number of picks) were all profitable, so it is not one lucky setting.
  • Every weekday and every market mood was profitable, and the effect was as strong in 2025–2026 as in 2017–2019.

What can go wrong

  • These are test results, not a promise. No real or paper order has been placed yet.
  • About 4 nights in 10 lose. The profit comes from winning nights being a little bigger and a little more frequent.
  • 2022 was a losing year in every version.
  • The honest list is not filtered for halal, because nobody recorded which companies were halal in 2017. It proves the effect; real trading would use the halal list.
  • Whole shares only. Auction orders cannot buy part of a share, so a small account cannot follow every pick. About $5,000 is the practical minimum.
  • The results assume the official opening and closing prices. That is what auction orders give, but it has not been checked with real orders.
  • A known effect can fade once many people trade it.
